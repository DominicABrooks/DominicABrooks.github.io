I passed the ISTQB Certified Tester Foundation Level (CTFL) exam on November 14th with an 87.5% (you need 65% to pass). I studied for about a week and had a lot of fun with it. Shoutout to my employer, Aspida, for paying for the exam.

I just got a congratulatory letter from Margaret Murry, the Certification Director at ASTQB, so it feels official enough now to make a blog post about it.

The part I liked most was the vocabulary. CTFL gives exact names to things like equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, decision coverage and risk-based testing, so everyone who's taken it means the same thing by them. That makes it a lot easier to talk about testing with people from other teams and companies.

My favorite of the seven testing principles is "testing is context dependent." The syllabus describes a fully staffed test organization, with dedicated test managers, test analysts and technical test analysts. In practice, teams adapt that to their size, and one QA engineer often covers several of those roles (manual testing, automation, test planning, sometimes DevOps). I treat the processes as a menu of proven practices and pick the ones that fit the team, which is exactly what that principle encourages.

Next I'm going for the Advanced Test Automation Engineer (TAE) certification. It's much closer to my day-to-day work, so I'm curious whether it'll feel even more practical.

If you're in QA and thinking about CTFL, I'd say go for it.
